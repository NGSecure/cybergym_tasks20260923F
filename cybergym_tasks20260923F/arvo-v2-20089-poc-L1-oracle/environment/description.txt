In nDPI's packet-classification example application, AddressSanitizer
reports a heap-buffer-overflow READ of 2 bytes while the per-packet
processing entry point inspects the captured frame's data-link header to
determine the network-layer protocol type. The routine reads a 16-bit
protocol-type field directly out of the raw captured packet buffer, at the
offset where the data-link header says that field should live, without first
confirming that the number of bytes actually captured for this packet is
large enough to contain a full link-layer header of that size.

A crafted capture whose per-packet captured length is shorter than a minimal
link-layer header causes this field read to land past the end of the heap
buffer holding the packet's raw bytes. The fix adds an early length check at
the very top of the per-packet processing entry point that bails out of all
further processing whenever the packet's captured length falls below a fixed
minimum, before any data-link-specific parsing is attempted.
